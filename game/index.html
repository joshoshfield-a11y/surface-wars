How to Play

New here? Tutorial on the main menu teaches the core moves by doing, one step at a time, with nobody shooting at you. Tutorial II teaches twin-stick control and the hero the same way. This page is the quick reference.

Goal

You are Iron Eden (blue). Wipe out every red unit and structure to win. Lose your Command Core and all your units, and you lose.

Select

Tap a blue unit to select it. The top bar shows your current mode, and the feedback line under the map always says what your next tap will do in that mode, so a tap is never a surprise. On a phone, drag pans the camera by default — turn on Select (in the View & selection row) and drag draws a selection box instead. To select many, use Select All, drag-select, or turn on Add Select and tap units one at a time. Double-tap a unit to select all of its type.

Move

With a unit selected, tap open ground to move it. A dashed line shows its path. Ground units cannot cross water — on the First Map the single bridge is the only crossing; on the Second Map (Twin Bridges) there are two bridges across a central lake, plus land routes around its north and south ends.

Attack

Tap a red unit or structure to attack it, in any mode. Attack-Move sends units toward a point and lets them fight anything they meet on the way.

Build

Tap your Command Core to open production. Tap a unit to queue it; scrap is paid at once, and Cancel refunds it in full. New units appear next to the core.

Salvage

Dead units leave wrecks worth scrap (40% of the unit's cost, shrinking over 120 seconds). Select a unit, then tap a wreck to collect it. Wrecks never block movement.

Camera

Landscape first: rotate your phone for the wide view — map on the left, commands in a side panel on the right, order buttons always visible. In portrait the game still works exactly as before (bottom tray; More commands opens groups, maps, and zoom) and a small pill suggests rotating — tap "Continue in portrait" to dismiss it for the session; it never blocks the map. Drag to pan. Pinch, or the Zoom buttons, to zoom (0.4×–3×). Tap the minimap to jump the camera there, or drag it to pan.

Groups and Queue

Groups: turn on Assign Group, tap a number to save the selection, tap the number again to recall it. Queue: turn on Queue, then tap several points to chain orders; tap Queue again to stop chaining. Patrol loops between two tapped points. Guard defends one point and returns to it after a fight; Hold just stops where a unit stands. Rally (in the core panel) sends new units to a tapped point on spawn.

New units

Your Iron Eden roster adds four mechs, built from the Command Core like any other unit: Bastion (heavy frontline anchor), Gatekeeper (laser skirmisher, ground and air), Deadeye (precision fire support, long reach, blind up close and blind to aircraft), and Mortarjack (mobile indirect fire over obstacles). The red Xandria roster adds four of its own: Needlewing (fast scout-striker), Longsight (long-vision spotter), Javelin (mobile assault for flanking), and Raptor (close assault). Each has a counter in its unit description — read it in the info panel before you build.

Twin-stick control and the hero

Select one armed unit and tap Take Control to drive it yourself: left stick (or WASD) moves, right stick (or mouse aim + hold) aims and fires. Release Control gives the unit a hold order where it stands and keeps it selected. The Command Mech hero starts each match in reserve, so the field opens 9 against 10: tap Drop Back In to drop it near your Command Core, where you immediately control it. Releasing the hero withdraws it to reserve with its HP kept; if it dies on the field, redeploying costs scrap after a cooldown. Tutorial II on the main menu teaches all of this by doing, with the red side held.

Hero mastery

Hero level caps at 10. Past level 10, mastery ranks M1–M5 at 5500, 6800, 8300, 10000, and 11900 total XP each grant one extra skill point at match end. Mastery grants no stats, no gear, and no second ability — it is skill points and prestige only.

Salvage Credits and the Premium Store

Salvage Credits are separate from match scrap and are never converted into it. You earn them only at match end: win 120, draw 60, loss 30, plus 10 per hero killing blow up to 10 kills. Quitting mid-match earns nothing, and a match with the hero toggled off earns nothing. The Premium Store on the main menu sells loadout packs for Salvage Credits only. Real-money purchases: not live — there is no checkout, no real-currency price, and no payment provider. Buying a pack you already own is impossible; a pack never used in a match refunds in full, and a pack used in a match cannot be refunded. Equipping a pack never bypasses the hero's level tier gates.

Not in this slice

Research/tech tree, fog of war, campaign, multiplayer, placing new structures, Power/Components/Supplies layers, Salvage Clans/Directorate factions, transport, and repair. The red side's combat AI defends its core, groups up before attacking, and falls damaged units back to defend — on the same scrap economy as you, with no free units or scrap. Difficulty (Easy / Normal / Hard, on the main menu) changes only that AI behaviour — how soon it attacks, how stubbornly it defends, and how quickly damaged units fall back — never costs, stats, or scrap, and never in a tutorial game. On desktop only: Shift+tap queues, Ctrl+number assigns a group, Esc/Space pauses, +/−/0/F/P are zoom and path keys.

How to win

You know the moves now: select, move, attack, build, salvage. In a real game the red side fights back, rebuilds, and salvages — so protect your Command Core, spend scrap on units, and grab wrecks before they decay.

Win by wiping out every red unit and structure. Lose if your core and all your units are gone. Draw if neither side can damage the other and neither side can build new units — the match ends in a draw after 30 seconds in that state, instead of stalling.

Tutorial II — last step

You can drive a unit, aim and fire, release it to a hold order, and drop the Command Mech back in. One step left: look at the Premium Store. It sells loadout packs for earned Salvage Credits only. Viewing only — it costs nothing and buys nothing.

Real-money purchases: not live. There is no checkout here.

Hero Loadout — Command Mech

Between matches only: this loadout is fixed for the match once it starts, and cannot be changed mid-match. "Command Mech" is a placeholder name — the final name is not decided yet.

Hero progress is stored only on this device — clearing app data (APK) or browser storage (web) loses it permanently, with no recovery. Progress on the phone does not appear on the laptop, and vice versa.

Skills: 1 point each, 1 point per level from level 2. Passive skills are always on once unlocked; the ability slot holds one equipped active skill. Respec is free between matches and returns all spent points. Skill effects and the XP curve are PROPOSED numbers, not playtested balance yet.

Tier 2 weapons and gear unlock at hero level 4, tier 3 at level 7. Higher tiers are shown locked with the level they need — never silently hidden.

Premium Store

Packs are curated hero loadouts, bought with Salvage Credits earned at match end (win 120, draw 60, loss 30, plus 10 per hero killing blow up to 10 kills). Credits are earned in matches only — they are not match scrap, and the two balances are never mixed.

Real-money purchases: not live. There is no checkout, no real-currency price, and no payment provider in this build — everything here is bought with earned Salvage Credits only.

Premium weapons are sidegrades with stated trade-offs, never a stat pile. Owning a pack never bypasses hero level gates: tier 2 items need level 4, tier 3 need level 7. A pack that has never been used in a match can be refunded for its full price; a pack used in a match cannot be refunded. Equipping a pack sets the hero loadout to the pack's loadout for the next match.

Victory

The game is frozen. Restart the map or return to the main menu.
