## What it is

Surface Wars is a strict 90° top-down real-time strategy game. You command
Iron Eden (blue) against Xandria (red) on large maps, including an 80×50
First Map with a river and a single bridge crossing. You gather scrap,
produce units from your Command Core, fight massed battles with mechs,
tanks, and artillery, and salvage the wrecks left behind.

## What it does (demonstrable in the current build)

- Strict 90° top-down view, pan, zoom, and minimap play.
- Landscape-first mobile play, with a portrait layout and a rotate hint.
- A 7-step interactive tutorial that teaches by playing: look around, select,
  move, attack, build, salvage, and the win condition.
- A second tutorial, Tutorial II — Twin-Stick & Hero, that teaches by playing:
  Take Control of a unit, drive it, aim and fire, release it to a hold order,
  Drop Back In for the Command Mech hero, and view a pack in the Premium Store
  (viewing only — no step requires a purchase).
- Three skirmish maps: First Map (80×50, river, single bridge), Second Map —
  Twin Bridges (80×50, central lake, two bridges), Third Map — Forest Belt
  (80×50).
- 8 new mechs: Iron Eden gains Bastion, Gatekeeper, Deadeye, and Mortarjack;
  Xandria gains Needlewing, Longsight, Javelin, and Raptor. Every new mech
  leaves a salvageable wreck, like every other unit.
- Twin-stick manual control: Take Control of one armed unit, drive with the
  left stick or WASD, aim and fire with the right stick or mouse, Release
  Control to a hold order with the selection kept.
- Command Mech hero: starts each match in reserve (field opens 9 against 10),
  drops in near your Command Core on Drop Back In, withdraws to reserve on
  release, persists between matches with levels (cap 10), skills, and gear
  tiers, and mastery ranks M1–M5 past level 10 that grant skill points only.
- Salvage Credits and the Premium Store (main menu only): earn Credits only
  at match end — win 120, draw 60, loss 30, plus 10 per hero killing blow up
  to 10 kills; quitting mid-match earns nothing. Packs cost Salvage Credits
  only. A pack never used in a match refunds in full; a pack used in a match
  cannot be refunded; equipping never bypasses hero level tier gates.
- Difficulty settings (Easy / Normal / Hard) that change only red AI
  behaviour — never costs, stats, or scrap.
- Scrap economy: start with 500 scrap, produce units at catalog cost and build
  time, cancel for a full refund.
- Persistent wrecks: destroyed units stay on the field as wrecks worth 40% of
  their build cost, decaying linearly to zero over 120 seconds. Any unit can
  salvage a wreck within range.
- Orders: move, attack-move, hold, patrol, guard, rally points, queued orders,
  formations, and on-screen unit groups.
- Red opponent runs the same scrap economy (production and salvage) and a
  real combat AI: it defends its core, groups units before attacking,
  splits attacks across routes, and falls damaged units back — with no
  free units, no free scrap, and no information the player could not see.

## What it does NOT do yet

- No research or tech tree.
- No fog of war.
- No campaign.
- No multiplayer.
- No placing new structures (production is from the Command Core only).
- No Power, Components, or Supplies economy layers (scrap only).
- No Salvage Clans or Directorate factions (Iron Eden and Xandria only).
- No transport or repair.
- Real-money purchases: not live. There is no checkout, no real-currency
  price, and no payment provider anywhere in the game or this draft.
- Pinch zoom has never been tested with a real finger; on-screen Zoom
  buttons are the verified zoom route on phones.
- Tutorial II has not been played on a real phone; its drive, aim, and
  drop steps are proven in headless desktop Chromium only, and the phone
  aim window (target visible while still outside minimum weapon range)
  is narrower than on desktop.
- No performance claim is made for any phone. Sandbox test numbers are not
  phone frame rates, and the game has not been benchmarked on a real device.

## Free taster (the demo)

The free taster is the tutorial plus the First Map skirmish: a new player
learns every core action in the tutorial, then plays a full skirmish against
the red side with the same economy. Nothing in the taster is staged in a way
the full build cannot match — it is the same game, with the red side held
still only inside the tutorial itself.
